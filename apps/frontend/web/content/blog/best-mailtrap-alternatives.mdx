## Why teams look for a Mailtrap alternative

Mailtrap started as the place developers send test email so it never reaches real users. It later added a production sending API and a marketing product. It's well built, and its testing sandbox is genuinely good. Teams look elsewhere for a few reasons:

- **Three products, three bills.** Email API/SMTP (sending), Email Sandbox (testing) and Email Marketing are priced separately, each with its own free tier.
- **Dedicated IPs mean Business.** A dedicated IP starts with the Business plan at $85 for 100,000 emails.
- **The sandbox free tier is small.** 50 test emails a month; paid sandbox plans start at $14 a month billed annually.
- **Marketing is contact-priced and separate.** The free tier allows 500 contacts and 1,500 emails a month.
- **Hosted only.** No source and no self-hosting.

If you're happy with both products, stay. If not, here are the options for each job.

## 1. Reloop: best overall for sending

Reloop is open-source email infrastructure: a transactional API, SMTP relay, inbound mailboxes, contacts, campaigns, automations and webhooks in one product. Use it hosted (Reloop Cloud runs on Hetzner, a German hosting company, with EU data centers) or run the same code on your own server.

**Pricing:** Free is 6,000 emails a month (200 a day). Pro is $10 for 50,000. Growth is $20 for 100,000 and includes a dedicated IP. Extra mail is $0.50 per 1,000. Or skip the subscription and buy pay-as-you-go credits at $0.50 per 1,000, valid for 6 months. Self-hosting is free under Apache 2.0.

**Strengths**

- **Half Mailtrap's price at 50k**: $10 vs $20, and $20 vs $30 at 100k.
- **A dedicated IP at $20**, instead of moving to an $85 Business plan.
- **Campaigns and event-triggered automations included**, with one suppression list for transactional and marketing mail. No separate contact-priced marketing product.
- **Inbound as real mailboxes.** Mail is stored, threaded, spam-scored with Rspamd, and readable over the API, the [MCP server](/docs/integrations/ai-tools/mcp-server) or IMAP. Hosted plans include 3, 5 or 10 inboxes on Free, Pro and Growth.
- Errors come back with `message`, `why` and `fix`.

**Weaknesses**

- **No capture-only test sandbox.** For staging, keep Mailtrap's sandbox or run Mailpit (below).
- Open and click tracking are on for new domains; switch them off per domain if you don't want them.

**Best for:** teams sending more than a few thousand emails a month who want sending, inboxes and campaigns in one place.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Mailtrap](/blog/reloop-vs-mailtrap).

## 2. Resend: closest developer experience

Resend made the clean, Stripe-style email API the norm, and its React Email project made JSX templates mainstream.

**Pricing:** Free is 3,000 a month (100 a day). Pro is $20 for 50,000 and $35 for 100,000. Overage is $0.90 per 1,000.

**Strengths:** excellent docs and onboarding, first-class React Email, a large ecosystem of examples, and broadcasts for simple marketing sends.

**Weaknesses:** the same as Mailtrap at 50k, and $35 at 100k vs Mailtrap's $30. Hosted only. Inbound is a webhook per message. A dedicated IP needs the $90 Scale plan plus a $30/mo add-on.

**Best for:** small teams that want a polished hosted API and expect to stay under 50k a month.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Resend](/blog/reloop-vs-resend).

## 3. Postmark: best transactional reputation

Postmark has been transactional-first for over a decade, and its **message streams** keep bulk mail from hurting your transactional reputation.

**Pricing:** 100 emails a month free. Basic is $15 for 10,000, then $1.80 per extra 1,000, so about $87 at 50k and $177 at 100k. Dedicated IPs are $50 a month for senders above 300,000 a month.

**Strengths:** strong reputation for inbox placement, message streams, clear docs.

**Weaknesses:** much more expensive than Mailtrap past 10k. Inbound needs Pro. Hosted only.

**Best for:** low-volume senders who value Postmark's reputation over price.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Postmark](/blog/reloop-vs-postmark).

## 4. MailerSend: best with a visual builder

MailerSend is a transactional API from the team behind MailerLite, with a drag-and-drop builder, SMS, email verification and split testing.

**Pricing:** Free is 500 emails a month. Hobby is $7 for 5,000. Starter is $35 for 50,000 and $68 for 100,000, with overage at $1.30 per 1,000. Professional adds the dedicated IP add-on.

**Strengths:** non-developers can edit templates, SMS and verification come from the same vendor, and DMARC and blocklist monitoring are included on Starter and up.

**Weaknesses:** more than Mailtrap at both 50k and 100k. Inbound mail is forwarded, not stored, and each forward counts against your quota. Hosted only.

**Best for:** teams where marketers or support staff edit transactional templates.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs MailerSend](/blog/reloop-vs-mailersend).

## 5. Mailgun: best for inbound routing

Mailgun is one of the original developer email APIs, with mature sending, email validation and **inbound routes** that match incoming mail by pattern and forward it.

**Pricing:** a trial at 100 emails a day. Basic is $15 for 10,000; Foundation is $35 for 50,000. Overage starts at $1.30 per 1,000. Dedicated IPs are a paid add-on.

**Strengths:** long track record, flexible inbound routing, built-in email validation, Handlebars templates.

**Weaknesses:** around $100 at 100k with overage. Marketing is a separate Sinch product. Hosted only.

**Best for:** teams that need flexible inbound routing rules.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Mailgun](/blog/reloop-vs-mailgun).

## 6. SendGrid: best for enterprise procurement

SendGrid (Twilio) is the incumbent, with a mature enterprise program, subusers, IP pools and full Handlebars templating.

**Pricing:** a 60-day trial at 100 emails a day. Essentials is $19.95 for 50,000 and $34.95 for 100,000. Dedicated IPs, subusers and SSO start on Pro from $89.95.

**Strengths:** procurement-friendly, mature compliance program, Handlebars logic in templates.

**Weaknesses:** no permanent free plan, marketing is on separate plans, inbound is the Inbound Parse webhook. Hosted only.

**Best for:** larger companies whose procurement prefers an established vendor.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs SendGrid](/blog/reloop-vs-sendgrid).

## 7. Mailpit: best free testing sandbox

If you use Mailtrap for its sandbox, the closest free replacement is [Mailpit](https://github.com/axllent/mailpit), an MIT-licensed email testing tool. It runs an SMTP server that captures everything you send, with a web UI to inspect it. It's the actively maintained successor in spirit to MailHog, which no longer gets updates.

**Pricing:** free and open source.

**Strengths**

- A single static binary or a Docker image; the web UI is on port `8025` and SMTP on `1025` by default.
- A REST API for automated integration tests, so CI can assert that an email was sent and check its contents.
- HTML compatibility scoring across mail clients, link checking and SpamAssassin spam scoring.

**Weaknesses**

- You run it yourself, per developer or per environment. There's no shared hosted inbox for the whole team out of the box.
- It's a testing tool only; it doesn't deliver mail to real inboxes in production.

**Best for:** local development and CI. Point your app's staging SMTP settings at Mailpit, and production at your real sender.

  You don't have to replace both products at once. A common setup is a capture tool (Mailtrap's sandbox or Mailpit) for staging and a production sender for real users. Keep the two configured by environment so staging can never email a customer.
</Callout>

## Switching from Mailtrap sending

1. **Add your domain** in the new provider and publish its DKIM record. Merge the new SPF include into your existing record; never publish a second SPF record.
2. **Swap the client.** On Reloop, replace `mailtrap.send` with `reloop.mail.send`, turn `{ name, email }` objects into `"Name <email>"` strings, and check `emailError` instead of catching.
3. **Move templates.** On Reloop, change placeholders to triple braces: `{{{user_name}}}`.
4. **Import your suppression list** so you don't re-mail addresses that bounced or complained.
5. **Point webhooks** at a new handler. Reloop signs events with HMAC-SHA256 in the `Reloop-Signature` header.
6. **Split environments.** Staging SMTP points at your sandbox (Mailtrap or Mailpit); production points at `smtp.reloop.sh` on port 587 with STARTTLS, using your API key as both username and password.
7. **Ramp traffic** from 10% to 100% over a week or two, then remove Mailtrap's sending DNS records.

The [Reloop vs Mailtrap](/blog/reloop-vs-mailtrap) post has the detailed version, and the [SendGrid migration guide](/blog/migrating-from-sendgrid) shows a traffic-split flag that works for any provider.
