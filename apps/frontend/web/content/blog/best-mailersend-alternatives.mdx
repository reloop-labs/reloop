**The short version:** if you're leaving MailerSend for price, **Reloop** is $10 for 50,000 emails where MailerSend Starter is $35, stores inbound mail in real inboxes instead of forwarding it, and is open source. **Resend** is the closest pure-API alternative. ## Why teams look for a MailerSend alternative

MailerSend is a solid developer-first API with a better no-code side than most: a drag-and-drop builder, SMS, email verification and split testing. Teams usually look elsewhere for one of these reasons:

- **Price climbs with volume.** Starter is $35 for 50,000 and $68 for 100,000, with overage at $1.30 per 1,000.
- **The free plan is small.** 500 emails a month, 1 domain, 1 template, 1 webhook and 100 API requests a day.
- **Inbound is forwarding, not storage.** Incoming mail is parsed and forwarded to a webhook or address, and each forward counts against your monthly quota.
- **Dedicated IPs need Professional.** The add-on is on the $110 plan.
- **Marketing lives in a sister product.** Campaigns and automations are MailerLite's job, a separate account.
- **Hosted only.** No source, no self-hosting.

## 1. Reloop: best overall MailerSend alternative

Reloop is open-source email infrastructure: a transactional API, SMTP relay, inbound mailboxes, contacts, campaigns, automations and webhooks in one product. Use it hosted (Reloop Cloud runs on Hetzner, a German hosting company, with EU data centers) or run the same code on your own server.

**Pricing:** Free is 6,000 emails a month (200 a day). Pro is $10 for 50,000. Growth is $20 for 100,000 and includes a dedicated IP. Extra mail is $0.50 per 1,000. Self-hosting is free under Apache 2.0.

**Strengths**

- **$10 vs $35 at 50k, $20 vs $68 at 100k.** Over a year at 100k that's $576 saved against Starter.
- **Twelve times the free tier**: 6,000 a month vs 500.
- **Inbound is stored, not forwarded.** Mail lands in threaded mailboxes, spam-scored with Rspamd, readable over the API, the [MCP server](/docs/integrations/ai-tools/mcp-server) or IMAP, and replies stay in-thread. Hosted plans include 3, 5 or 10 inboxes on Free, Pro and Growth.
- **Marketing in the same product.** Contacts, groups, subscription channels, campaigns and event-triggered automations share one API key and one suppression list. No second account.
- A dedicated IP at $20 instead of a Professional add-on.

**Weaknesses**

- No drag-and-drop email builder. Templates are HTML or code (React Email works), with `{{{variable}}}` placeholders.
- No SMS, built-in email verification or split testing.
- Younger product, smaller community.

**Best for:** developer teams that want lower prices, real inboxes and campaigns without a second tool.

## 2. Resend: best pure developer API

Resend set the standard for a modern email API, and React Email made JSX templates mainstream.

**Pricing:** Free is 3,000 a month (100 a day). Pro is $20 for 50,000 and $35 for 100,000. Overage is $0.90 per 1,000.

**Strengths:** excellent docs, first-class React Email, a large ecosystem, and broadcasts for simple marketing sends.

**Weaknesses:** a dedicated IP needs the $90 Scale plan plus a $30/mo add-on. No drag-and-drop builder. Inbound is a webhook per message. Hosted only.

**Best for:** small developer teams under 50k emails a month who want the most polished hosted API.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Resend](/blog/reloop-vs-resend).

## 3. Postmark: best for transactional deliverability

Postmark has been transactional-first for over a decade, and its **message streams** keep bulk mail from affecting transactional reputation.

**Pricing:** a free developer plan with 100 emails a month. Basic is $15 for 10,000, with overage at $1.80 per 1,000 ($1.30 on Pro, $1.20 on Platform). Inbound needs Pro. Dedicated IPs are $50 a month per IP above 300,000 emails a month.

**Strengths:** the longest transactional-only track record, widely praised support, mature docs.

**Weaknesses:** at 50k, Basic with overage is about $87 vs MailerSend Starter's $35. No contact database or automation builder. Hosted only.

**Best for:** low-volume senders where a late password reset costs real money.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Postmark](/blog/reloop-vs-postmark).

## 4. Brevo: best all-in-one marketing suite

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) is a marketing platform with a transactional API attached: email, SMS, WhatsApp, CRM and live chat in one product.

**Pricing:** free for 300 emails a day. Starter begins at $9 for 5,000 emails; Standard, which adds automations, starts at $18. Larger volumes are priced on a slider.

**Strengths:** marketers can run everything without developers, and SMS and WhatsApp sit next to email. If you'd otherwise pair MailerSend with MailerLite, Brevo covers both.

**Weaknesses:** inbound is parsed to a webhook. Dedicated IPs are on higher tiers. Hosted only.

**Best for:** marketing-led teams that want one suite for every channel.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Brevo](/blog/reloop-vs-brevo).

## 5. Mailjet: best visual editor

Mailjet, owned by Sinch, pairs a transactional API with one of the better drag-and-drop editors, and stores data in the EU.

**Pricing:** free for 6,000 emails a month (200 a day). At 50,000 it's $40 on Essential or $59 on Premium; 100,000 on Premium is $112. Automations need Premium.

**Strengths:** tied with Reloop for the largest free tier here, a strong visual editor, transactional and marketing in one account.

**Weaknesses:** gets expensive at 100k. Dedicated IPs are on request at higher volumes. Inbound is the Parse API, posting each message to your webhook. Hosted only.

**Best for:** teams that want MailerSend's builder-friendly approach with a bigger free tier.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Mailjet](/blog/reloop-vs-mailjet).

## 6. Mailgun: best for inbound routing rules

Mailgun is one of the original developer email APIs, with **inbound routes** that match incoming mail by pattern and forward it, plus email validation.

**Pricing:** a trial at 100 emails a day. Basic is $15 for 10,000; Foundation is $35 for 50,000. Overage starts at $1.30 per 1,000 ($1.80 on Basic). Dedicated IPs are a paid add-on.

**Strengths:** long track record, flexible routing, Handlebars templates.

**Weaknesses:** about $100 at 100k with overage. Marketing is a separate Sinch product. Hosted only.

**Best for:** teams that route a lot of inbound mail by rule.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Mailgun](/blog/reloop-vs-mailgun).

## 7. Elastic Email: best budget hosted option

Elastic Email sells an Email API and a separate Email Marketing product, both with simple plans.

**Pricing:** the Email API's free tier is 3,000 a month (100 a day). At 50,000 it's $19 on Starter or $49 on Pro. Higher volumes use a slider. A dedicated IP is a $40 add-on on Starter and $50 on Pro.

**Strengths:** cheaper than MailerSend Starter at 50k, and its separate Email Marketing product has a drag-and-drop designer.

**Weaknesses:** inbound and webhooks are Pro only. Logs are kept 3 days on Free and Starter, 7 on Pro. Marketing is a separate plan. Hosted only.

**Best for:** budget-conscious senders who want a hosted dashboard and don't need inbound.

Full comparison: [Reloop vs Elastic Email](/blog/reloop-vs-elastic-email).

## Switching from MailerSend

1. **Add your domain** in the new provider and publish its DKIM record. Merge the new SPF include into your existing record; don't add a second SPF record.
2. **Swap the client.** MailerSend's Node SDK uses a builder (`new EmailParams().setFrom(...)`); most alternatives take a plain object.
3. **Move templates.** The HTML moves as-is. On Reloop, change `{{variable}}` placeholders to `{{{variable}}}` and send by template ID.
4. **Export your suppression list** and import it.
5. **Point webhooks** at a new handler. MailerSend events are `activity.*`; Reloop's are `email.delivered`, `email.bounced` and so on, signed in the `Reloop-Signature` header.
6. **Move inbound routes.** On Reloop, create a mailbox for the address instead of a forward rule.
7. **Ramp traffic** from 10% to 100% over a week or two, then remove MailerSend's DNS records.

See [Reloop vs MailerSend](/blog/reloop-vs-mailersend) for the details, and the [SendGrid migration guide](/blog/migrating-from-sendgrid) for a traffic-split flag you can reuse.
