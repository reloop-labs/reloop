**The short version:** if you chose useSend because it's open source but you want inbound email, no AWS dependency when self-hosting, or a lower bill below a few hundred thousand emails a month, **Reloop** is the closest alternative. **Plunk** is the other open-source platform built on SES. **Postal** is a mature self-hosted mail server, and **Listmonk** is the pick for self-hosted newsletters.

useSend (formerly Unsend) is a well-made open-source Resend alternative, and we respect the project. Teams usually look elsewhere for one of these reasons:

- **Self-hosting means Amazon SES.** You need an AWS account, an IAM user, SNS callbacks, SES sandbox approval and GitHub OAuth before sending a single email.
- **No inbound email yet.** Its README lists receiving mail as a roadmap item.
- **Still in beta.** That matters if you need stability guarantees for password resets.
- **Marketing mail costs more.** On the hosted plan, marketing email is $1.00 per 1,000, against $0.40 for transactional.

We build Reloop, so weigh this accordingly. Facts and prices come from our researched head-to-head posts and each project's own site, and each section says where that alternative beats us.

## 1. Reloop: best overall useSend alternative

[Reloop](/) is open-source email infrastructure under Apache 2.0: a transactional API, SMTP relay, inbound mailboxes, contacts, campaigns, automations and webhooks. Like useSend, it has a Resend-style API and a hosted cloud. Unlike useSend, it ships its own mail server.

**Pricing:** Reloop Cloud is free for 6,000 emails a month (200 a day), $10 for 50,000 (Pro) and $20 for 100,000 with a dedicated IP (Growth), then $0.50 per 1,000. Marketing and transactional mail cost the same. Self-hosting has no per-email fee.

**Strengths**

- **No AWS when self-hosting.** Reloop sends through KumoMTA on your server: no IAM user, no SES sandbox review, no per-email fee to anyone. Install with [one command](/self-host) on a server with 2 vCPUs and 4 GB of RAM.
- **Inbound mail into real inboxes**, threaded, spam-scored with Rspamd, and readable over the API, the [MCP server](/docs/integrations/ai-tools/mcp-server) or IMAP. Built for AI agents and support teams.
- **Cheaper hosted below about 250k emails a month**, and every email costs the same.
- **Event-triggered automations** with delays and conditions, plus campaigns and contacts.
- **Apache 2.0**, so there's no copyleft obligation if you modify and host it. Reloop Cloud runs on Hetzner, a German hosting company, with EU data centers.

**Weaknesses**

- **You own IP reputation when self-hosting.** Warm-up and blocklist monitoring are your job, where SES handles it for useSend.
- At millions of transactional emails a month on hosted plans, useSend's $0.40 per 1,000 beats our $0.50 overage.

**Best for:** teams that want open source without an AWS dependency, or need inbound mail today. Full comparison: [Reloop vs useSend](/blog/reloop-vs-usesend).

## 2. Plunk: best open source on SES

[Plunk](https://www.useplunk.com) is an open-source email platform under AGPL-3.0 with a transactional API, SMTP, contacts, campaigns, workflows and inbound email with routing rules.

**Pricing:** free for 1,000 emails a month with Plunk branding, then $0.001 per email ($1 per 1,000) with no base fee and a monthly spend cap.

**Strengths:** the simplest pricing around, automations and inbound already built, and SES handles deliverability when self-hosted.

**Weaknesses:** self-hosting needs the same AWS setup as useSend, and at steady volume $1 per 1,000 costs more than flat plans.

**Best for:** low or spiky volume, and teams happy on SES that want automations useSend doesn't list. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Plunk](/blog/reloop-vs-plunk).

## 3. Postal: best pure self-hosted mail server

[Postal](https://github.com/postalserver/postal) is a mature, MIT-licensed mail delivery platform written in Ruby. It has an HTTP API, SMTP, webhooks, IP pools with routing rules, and inbound forwarding to an HTTP endpoint, SMTP server or address.

**Pricing:** free and self-hosted only. Its minimum is 2 CPU cores, 4 GB of RAM and 25 GB of disk, with MariaDB 10.6 or newer.

**Strengths:** no AWS, battle-tested, flexible IP pools, permissive MIT license.

**Weaknesses:** no hosted option, no contacts, campaigns, automations or stored templates. It's a sending server, not a platform.

**Best for:** teams that want only an MTA they control. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Postal](/blog/reloop-vs-postal).

## 4. Listmonk: best self-hosted newsletters

[Listmonk](https://listmonk.app) is a newsletter and mailing list manager under AGPLv3, a single Go binary plus Postgres. It hands each message to an SMTP server you provide.

**Pricing:** free and self-hosted only; you pay for the server and your SMTP provider.

**Strengths:** very fast and light, SQL segmentation, a mature campaign UI, and a template-based transactional endpoint (`POST /api/tx`).

**Weaknesses:** doesn't send mail itself, no drip sequences, no inbound.

**Best for:** self-hosted newsletters. Many teams pair it with an SMTP backend such as Reloop. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Listmonk](/blog/reloop-vs-listmonk).

---

## 5. Resend: best hosted developer experience

[Resend](https://resend.com) is the hosted API useSend is modeled on, from the team that created React Email.

**Pricing:** free for 3,000 a month (100 a day), $20 for 50k, $35 for 100k, then $0.90 per 1,000.

**Strengths:** polished API and docs, React Email, a large ecosystem of examples.

**Weaknesses:** closed source and hosted only, inbound is a webhook, and the price climbs fast past 50k.

**Best for:** teams that left useSend for stability and don't need open source. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Resend](/blog/reloop-vs-resend). See also [Resend alternative](/alternatives/resend).

---

## 6. Amazon SES: cheapest raw pipe

[Amazon SES](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/) is what useSend, Plunk and Resend send through. Going to SES directly cuts out the platform layer.

**Pricing:** $200 in AWS credits for the first 6 months, then $0.10 per 1,000 plus $0.12 per GB outbound. About $10 for 100k.

**Strengths:** the lowest per-email price, and Amazon manages IP reputation.

**Weaknesses:** no dashboard, templates editor, contacts or inbox. Bounces arrive through SNS, and you build everything else.

**Best for:** AWS teams with time to build their own layer. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Amazon SES](/blog/reloop-vs-aws-ses). See also [Amazon SES alternative](/alternatives/aws-ses).

## How to choose

- **"We want open source but not AWS."** Reloop or Postal. Reloop if you also want campaigns, automations and inboxes; Postal if you only want an MTA.
- **"We need to receive and reply to email."** Reloop, with stored mailboxes, MCP and IMAP.
- **"We're happy on SES and want automations now."** Plunk.
- **"We mainly send newsletters."** Listmonk, with any SMTP backend.
- **"We just want something stable and hosted."** Reloop Cloud or Resend.
- **"Lowest possible cost per email."** Amazon SES directly.

## Switching from useSend

1. **Add your domain** in the new provider and publish its DKIM record. Merge its SPF include into your existing record rather than adding a second one.
2. **Swap the client.** useSend's `emails.send` maps closely to Reloop's `reloop.mail.send`; rename camelCase fields such as `replyTo` to `reply_to`.
3. **Move templates.** In Reloop, variables use triple braces: `{{{first_name}}}`.
4. **Export contacts and suppressions** and import them, so you don't email anyone who bounced or unsubscribed.
5. **Update webhooks.** Reloop signs events with HMAC-SHA256 in the `Reloop-Signature` header instead of `X-UseSend-Signature`.
6. **Ramp traffic** over one to two weeks, then remove the old SES identities and DNS records.
