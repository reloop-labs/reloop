## The welcome email is your highest-leverage send

Your welcome email is opened by ~80% of new signups. Nothing else you send will get that attention. Most teams waste it on a generic "Thanks for signing up" message. The best teams treat it as the beginning of an activation sequence.

## What a welcome sequence should accomplish

By the end of your welcome sequence (typically 5–7 days), a user should have:
1. Completed their first key action (your activation event)
2. Understood the top 2–3 things Reloop can do for them
3. Had at least one touchpoint with a human

## The 5-email sequence

### Email 1 — Immediate: The warm welcome
Send within 60 seconds of signup. Include:
- A genuine thank you (not corporate-speak)
- One thing to do right now (not a list of 10)
- A face and name — personal sender beats noreply@

### Email 2 — Day 1: The quick win
Show them how to get value in 5 minutes. Video > text. Numbered steps > prose.

### Email 3 — Day 3: Social proof
A case study or quote from a customer who faced the same problem they signed up to solve. Specificity wins here — "a startup like yours" is better than "thousands of customers."

### Email 4 — Day 5: Feature spotlight
Surface one advanced feature they probably haven't discovered yet. Frame it around the outcome, not the feature itself.

### Email 5 — Day 7: Personal check-in
Send from a real person with a real reply-to. Ask one question: "What's stopping you from [activation event]?" Replies become your best product feedback.

## Subject line patterns that work

- Curiosity: "One thing most people miss in Reloop"
- Specificity: "Your domain is verified — here's what to do next"
- Personalization: "[First name], your first email is 3 steps away"

## Building this with Reloop

Use Reloop's API to enqueue the sequence at signup and pause/skip emails based on user actions. If they complete the activation event after email 2, skip email 3's call-to-action and pivot to a feature discovery message instead.
