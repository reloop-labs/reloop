## First: what "GDPR-friendly" actually means

This isn't legal advice. Talk to your DPO or counsel for your own situation. But a few points clear up most of the confusion.

1. **GDPR doesn't require EU hosting.** It requires a lawful basis for processing, a data processing agreement (DPA) with your email provider, and a valid transfer mechanism whenever personal data leaves the EEA.
2. **Email providers process personal data.** Recipient addresses, names in templates and event logs are all personal data, so your provider is a processor and you need a signed DPA and a list of its sub-processors.
3. **Transfers outside the EEA need a mechanism.** The usual ones are an adequacy decision (such as the EU-US Data Privacy Framework for certified US companies) or Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) plus a transfer risk assessment.
4. **Schrems II is why teams still care.** In 2020 the EU's top court struck down Privacy Shield and said SCCs alone aren't enough if the destination country's surveillance laws undermine them. The Data Privacy Framework replaced Privacy Shield, but it has faced legal challenges, and many teams prefer not to depend on it.
5. **Location and jurisdiction are different questions.** Data in a Frankfurt data center run by a US company can still be subject to US law (the CLOUD Act). If that's your concern, look at who owns the provider and its hosting, not only where the servers are.

That gives you three levels to choose from:

- **EU-hosted:** servers are in the EU.
- **EU-hosted and EU-owned:** the provider and its hosting are European companies.
- **Self-hosted:** you run it, so you decide both.

## 1. Lettermint: best EU-owned, EU-hosted API

[Lettermint](https://lettermint.co/) is a Dutch company (Lettermint B.V.) whose site says it is "built in the Netherlands" and "hosted in Europe". It covers transactional email, broadcasts and inbound in one product, with a REST API and SMTP relay.

**Pricing:** free for 300 emails a month. Paid plans come in Starter, Growth and Pro, each priced by volume: 10,000 emails is €10, €13 or €15 a month, and 50,000 is €40, €45 or €55. A dedicated IP is €50 a month on Pro at 300,000 emails and up. Paid plans share one monthly volume across transactional, broadcast and inbound mail.

**Strengths**

- **European company, European hosting.** The simplest answer if your security review asks both "where" and "who".
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certified**, which shortens vendor questionnaires.
- Transactional, broadcast and inbound on one plan.
- SDKs and integrations for Node, PHP, Python, Go and Laravel, among others.

**Weaknesses**

- More expensive than Reloop Cloud per email: €40 vs $10 at 50k.
- Inbound mail is delivered by webhook, not stored as mailboxes.
- Hosted-only.

**Best for:** EU companies that want a modern hosted API and a clean answer on data sovereignty.

## 2. Sweego: best French-hosted option with SMS

[Sweego](https://sweego.io/) is a French email and SMS platform from the team behind Mindbaz, a long-running French bulk-email company. Sweego says its infrastructure runs on OVHcloud and Scaleway across several data centers in France.

**Pricing:** free for 100 emails a day, with webhooks and 7 days of logs. Individual starts at €10 a month for 20,000 to 50,000 emails. Startup starts at €79 for 100,000 to 200,000, with a dedicated IP available. Scaleup starts at €270 for 500,000 to 1 million and includes a dedicated IP. Extra IPs are €30 a month, and annual billing saves 20%. SMS is sold as credits, from €20.

**Strengths**

- **Hosted in France on French cloud providers**, a strong answer for French public-sector and regulated buyers.
- Email and SMS in one account.
- Log retention grows with the plan: 30 days on Individual, 60 on Startup, 90 on Scaleup.
- API and SMTP on every paid plan.

**Weaknesses**

- Focused on transactional email and SMS; no marketing campaigns or automations.
- Its documentation and community are smaller than the bigger names'.
- Hosted-only.

**Best for:** French and EU teams that want transactional email and SMS from one French provider.

## 3. MailPace: best privacy-first transactional

[MailPace](https://mailpace.com/) is made in the UK and, in its own words, "100% European Union hosted". Its stance is the most opinionated here: **"We never track email opens or clicks."** Instead of tracking pixels, it only reports whether the receiving server accepted the message.

**Pricing:** from $10 a month for the first 10,000 emails, scaling with a volume slider. Every feature is on every plan. There's an unadvertised free plan of up to 100 emails a month; you request it from support.

**Strengths**

- **No tracking pixels at all.** If your privacy policy or works council objects to open tracking, this removes the question.
- Inbound email turned into JSON webhooks.
- Idempotent requests and a simple HTTP API or SMTP.
- SDKs for Ruby, JavaScript, PHP, Go, Rust and Erlang.

**Weaknesses**

- **Transactional only.** MailPace prohibits marketing and newsletters.
- Shared IPs only; no dedicated or static IPs.
- At most 50 recipients per send.
- No open or click data, which some product teams do want.

**Best for:** privacy-focused products that send only transactional mail.

## 4. Reloop (self-hosted): best for full control of where data lives

[Reloop](/self-host) is open-source email infrastructure under Apache 2.0: a transactional API, SMTP relay, inbound mailboxes, contacts, campaigns, automations and webhooks. You can run the same code as our hosted service on your own server.

**Where Reloop Cloud is hosted:** we don't publish a hosting region for Reloop Cloud today, so we won't claim one here. What we do publish:

- A [Data Processing Addendum](/dpa) that relies on the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (including the UK Addendum) for any transfer outside the EEA.
- A [sub-processor list](/dpa/subprocessors). Reloop Cloud runs on infrastructure Reloop Labs operates itself, so the systems that touch customer data (PostgreSQL, Redis, NATS, MinIO, ClickHouse, KumoMTA, Rspamd and so on) are our own deployments, not third-party SaaS.

If you need data that provably stays in the EU, **self-host instead**:

```bash
curl -fsSL https://reloop.sh/install.sh | sudo bash
```

Run that on an EU VPS (Hetzner, OVHcloud, Scaleway or your own hardware). Point attachments and template images at EU object storage, such as Hetzner Object Storage or a MinIO box you run. Then you decide where everything lives, and no third party processes your mail.

Your application code doesn't change. Point the SDK at your own server:

```typescript
import { Reloop } from "reloop-email";

const reloop = new Reloop({
  apiKey: process.env.RELOOP_API_KEY!,
  baseUrl: "https://mail.example.eu",
});
```

**Pricing:** self-hosting is free; you pay for the server. The [VPS guide](/docs/self-host/vps) lists a minimum of 2 vCPUs and 4 GB of RAM. Hosted Reloop Cloud is Free (3,000 a month), Pro $10 for 50,000, and Growth $20 for 100,000 with a dedicated IP.

**Strengths**

- **You choose both location and jurisdiction.** No provider in between.
- Full platform: transactional, campaigns, event automations and inbound mailboxes with API, MCP and IMAP access.
- No per-email fee when self-hosted.
- Open source, so security reviewers can read the code that handles your users' mail.

**Weaknesses**

- **No published EU region for the hosted service.** If you want EU hosting without running servers, pick Lettermint, Sweego or MailPace.
- Self-hosting means you own IP reputation, warm-up, backups and uptime.

**Best for:** teams that need data on infrastructure they control, or that want one open-source platform for transactional and marketing mail. See [self-hosted email infrastructure](/blog/self-hosted-email-infrastructure) for what running it really involves.

## 5. Brevo: best EU-based marketing suite

[Brevo](https://www.brevo.com/) (formerly Sendinblue) runs from Paris. Its [data storage page](https://help.brevo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001005510-Data-storage-location) says its servers are in the EU, on OVH in France and Germany and on Google Cloud in Belgium.

**Pricing:** free for 300 emails a day. Starter begins at $9 for 5,000 emails, and Standard, which adds automations, starts at $18. Larger volumes are priced on a slider.

**Strengths:** email, SMS, WhatsApp, CRM and live chat in one product, with a drag-and-drop editor marketers can use without developers.

**Weaknesses:** gets expensive at higher volumes compared with developer-focused APIs, and inbound mail is a webhook, not a mailbox. Part of its hosting is on Google Cloud, a US company, if jurisdiction matters to you.

**Best for:** marketing-led teams in the EU. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Brevo](/blog/reloop-vs-brevo).

## 6. Mailjet: best established EU-hosted ESP

[Mailjet](https://www.mailjet.com/) started in France and is now owned by Sinch (Sweden). Its [security and privacy page](https://www.mailjet.com/security-privacy/) says customer data is stored in Google Cloud data centers in Frankfurt (Germany) and Saint-Ghislain (Belgium).

**Pricing:** free for 6,000 emails a month (200 a day). At 50,000 a month it's $40 on Essential or $59 on Premium; 100,000 on Premium is $112.

**Strengths:** long track record with GDPR, a strong drag-and-drop editor, and transactional and marketing in one account.

**Weaknesses:** automations need Premium, and dedicated IPs are on request at higher volumes. The hosting is EU-located but runs on Google Cloud.

**Best for:** teams that want a mature EU-hosted ESP with a good visual editor. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Mailjet](/blog/reloop-vs-mailjet).

## How to choose

- **"Our security review asks where the data is *and* who can be compelled to hand it over."** Lettermint or Sweego, or self-hosted Reloop.
- **"We can't use tracking pixels."** MailPace. Reloop also leaves open and click tracking off unless you enable it per domain.
- **"We need marketing campaigns too."** Brevo or Mailjet hosted, Lettermint for broadcasts, or self-hosted Reloop for campaigns plus automations.
- **"We need inbound mail as real mailboxes, for support or AI agents."** Self-hosted Reloop.
- **"We want the lowest cost and don't strictly need EU hosting."** Reloop Cloud at $10 for 50k, with a DPA and SCCs.

Whichever you choose, ask for three documents before signing: the **DPA**, the **sub-processor list**, and a statement of **where data is stored and backed up**. A provider that can't produce all three quickly is telling you something.

## Switching to an EU provider

The mechanics are the same for every provider here:

1. **Sign the DPA** and record the provider in your records of processing.
2. **Verify your domain.** Add the new DKIM record, merge the provider's include into your existing SPF record (never publish a second SPF record), and keep DMARC in place. Our [SPF, DKIM and DMARC guide](/blog/spf-dkim-dmarc-setup-guide) covers it.
3. **Export your suppression list** from the old provider and import it, so you don't email people who bounced or unsubscribed.
4. **Swap the client and templates**, then move webhooks to the new event names.
5. **Ramp traffic** over one to two weeks and watch bounce and complaint rates.
6. **Delete data at the old provider** once you're done, and ask for written confirmation.
