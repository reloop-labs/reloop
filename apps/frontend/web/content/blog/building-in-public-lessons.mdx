## Why we build in public

Building in public forces clarity. When you have to explain what you're working on and why, you can't hide behind vague roadmap items. It also builds an audience before you have a product, and creates accountability that solo or small-team projects often lack.

## Lesson 1: Ship something embarrassingly early

The first version of Reloop was a Docker Compose file and a basic SMTP relay. No dashboard, no API, no analytics. We shared it anyway. The feedback shaped every subsequent decision more than any internal planning session.

## Lesson 2: Documentation is marketing

Every time we wrote a "how to deploy on Coolify" or "how to configure DKIM" guide, we got inbound traffic from people who needed exactly that. Documentation compounds — it's the gift that keeps giving without ongoing effort.

## Lesson 3: GitHub stars lag real interest by weeks

When something gets shared on Hacker News or a relevant subreddit, stars spike 3–5 days later, not immediately. Don't judge the success of a post by the day-of numbers.

## Lesson 4: The community knows what to build next

Our most-requested features have consistently surprised us. Things we assumed were "nice to have" turned out to be table stakes for certain users. The issues tab and Discord are better product research than any framework.

## Lesson 5: Consistency beats intensity

Shipping once a month consistently, with updates and changelogs, compounds over time. A two-week sprint followed by silence loses momentum and community trust.

## What's next

We'll keep shipping in public. If you want to follow along, watch the [GitHub repo](https://github.com/reloop-labs/reloop), join the Discord, or subscribe to the blog. We're just getting started.
