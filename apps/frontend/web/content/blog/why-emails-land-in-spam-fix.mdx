## The spam filter is not your enemy

Spam filters are protecting your recipients — they're just doing it imperfectly. Understanding what triggers them lets you fix your sending practices rather than just trying to outsmart the filter.

## Reason 1: Missing or misconfigured authentication

This is the most common cause. If your domain doesn't have valid SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records, Gmail and Outlook will either reject or spam-folder your email immediately.

**Fix**: Set up all three. In that order. Check with `dig TXT yourdomain.com` after each one.

## Reason 2: Sending from a new IP with no reputation

A brand-new IP has zero reputation — ISPs are cautious about unknown senders.

**Fix**: Warm up your IP. Start with 50–100 emails/day to your most engaged recipients and ramp up over 4–6 weeks. See our [IP warm-up guide](/blog/ip-warming-guide) for a week-by-week schedule.

## Reason 3: High bounce rate

If more than 2% of your sends bounce, ISPs flag your domain as sending to bad data.

**Fix**: Never send to unverified or purchased lists. Remove addresses after the first hard bounce. Use double opt-in for marketing lists.

## Reason 4: Spam trigger words in subject lines

Words like "FREE!!!", "WINNER", "Act now", and excessive punctuation are red flags for Bayesian spam filters.

**Fix**: Write subject lines the way a human colleague would. Test with [mail-tester.com](https://mail-tester.com) before sending.

## Reason 5: Low engagement signals

ISPs track how recipients interact with your email. If recipients consistently ignore or delete your emails without opening them, your sender score drops.

**Fix**: Regularly clean your list. Remove subscribers who haven't opened in 90 days. It feels counterintuitive, but a smaller engaged list outperforms a large disengaged one.

## Reason 6: Sending from noreply@

`noreply@` tells ISPs that you're not interested in two-way communication — a signal associated with spam.

**Fix**: Send from a real address with a working reply-to. Even `hello@yourdomain.com` is dramatically better.

## How to diagnose your specific issue

1. Use [MXToolbox](https://mxtoolbox.com) to check your DNS records
2. Use [mail-tester.com](https://mail-tester.com) to get a spam score with specific issues flagged
3. Check Google Postmaster Tools for your domain's reputation score
4. Review Reloop's delivery logs for SMTP response codes — a 550 or 421 tells you a lot
