**The short version:** if you like Resend's API but want it cheaper, with real inboxes and the option to self-host, **Reloop** is the closest match: same API shape, $10 for 50k emails instead of $20, and open source. If you want open source on top of Amazon SES, look at **useSend**. If transactional deliverability is all you care about, **Postmark**. If you want the cheapest raw pipe and you're on AWS, **Amazon SES**.

Resend set the bar for a modern email API, and plenty of teams are happy on it. The reasons people look elsewhere are usually the same few:

- **Price at volume.** The free tier is generous (3,000 a month), but the next steps are $20 for 50k and $35 for 100k, with $0.90 per extra 1,000.
- **Hosted only.** There's no way to run Resend yourself, for data residency, security review or cost.
- **Inbound is a webhook.** Resend can receive mail and post each message to your webhook, but storage, threading and replies are yours to build.
- **Dedicated IPs** need the $90 Scale plan plus a $30/mo add-on.

We build Reloop, so weigh this accordingly. Every price below comes from our researched head-to-head posts and each provider's public pricing page, and each section says where that alternative beats us.

## 1. Reloop: best overall Resend alternative

[Reloop](/) is open-source email infrastructure: a transactional API, SMTP relay, inbound mailboxes, contacts, campaigns, automations and webhooks. Its API follows the same shape as Resend's on purpose, so switching is mostly a client swap.

```typescript
import { Reloop } from "reloop-email";

const reloop = new Reloop({ apiKey: process.env.RELOOP_API_KEY! });

const { response, emailError } = await reloop.mail.send({
  from: "Acme <hello@mail.acme.com>",
  to: "user@example.com",
  subject: "Welcome",
  html: "<p>Thanks for signing up.</p>",
});
```

**Pricing:** Free for 6,000 emails a month (200 a day), double Resend's free tier. Pro is $10 for 50,000, Growth is $20 for 100,000 with a dedicated IP, and extra mail is $0.50 per 1,000. Or skip the subscription and buy pay-as-you-go credits at $0.50 per 1,000, valid for 6 months. Self-hosting has no per-email fee.

**Strengths**

- **Half Resend's price at 50k and about 40% less at 100k.** Over a year at 100k a month, that's $180 saved, or $1,200 if you need a dedicated IP.
- **Inbound mail into real inboxes.** Messages are stored, threaded, spam-scored and readable over the API, the [MCP server](/docs/integrations/ai-tools/mcp-server) or IMAP, so AI agents and support teams can reply in-thread.
- **Transactional, campaigns and event automations** in one product with one suppression list.
- **Open source (Apache 2.0)** and self-hostable with a [one-command installer](/self-host). Reloop Cloud runs on Hetzner, a German hosting company, with EU data centers.
- **Dedicated IP on the $20 plan**, where Resend needs the $90 Scale plan plus a $30/mo add-on.
- **Errors that explain themselves**, with `why` and `fix` fields.

**Weaknesses**

- Smaller ecosystem of community examples than Resend.
- Hosted inboxes are capped by plan (3 on Free, 5 on Pro, 10 on Growth); self-hosted is unlimited.

**Best for:** teams past 50k emails a month, anyone building AI agents that handle email, and teams that want open source or self-hosting. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Resend](/blog/reloop-vs-resend). See also [Resend alternative](/alternatives/resend) and [compare Reloop and Resend](/compare/resend).

## 2. useSend: best open source on Amazon SES

[useSend](https://usesend.com) (formerly Unsend) is an open-source Resend alternative under AGPL-3.0. It wraps Amazon SES with a clean API, dashboard, contacts and campaigns, and offers a hosted cloud.

**Pricing:** Free for 3,000 a month (100 a day). Paid is usage-based with a $10 monthly minimum: $0.0004 per transactional email and $0.001 per marketing email.

**Strengths**

- Resend-style API and SDK, open source.
- At millions of transactional emails a month, $0.40 per 1,000 is hard to beat on a hosted plan.
- Self-hosting lets Amazon handle IP reputation.

**Weaknesses**

- Inbound email is on the roadmap, not shipped.
- Self-hosting needs an AWS account, SES sandbox approval, SNS callbacks and GitHub OAuth.
- Still marked beta.

**Best for:** teams already on AWS that want an open-source, SES-backed Resend. Full comparison: [Reloop vs useSend](/blog/reloop-vs-usesend).

## 3. Postmark: best for transactional deliverability

[Postmark](https://postmarkapp.com) has focused on transactional email for over a decade, with separate message streams so marketing mail never affects password resets.

**Pricing:** a developer plan with 100 emails a month. Basic is $15 for 10,000, then $1.80 per 1,000 ($87 at 50k, $177 at 100k at list price). Pro and Platform lower overage to $1.30 and $1.20.

**Strengths:** strong deliverability reputation, message streams, excellent docs and support.

**Weaknesses:** the most expensive option here per email, a tiny free tier, no contacts or automations, and hosted only.

**Best for:** teams where transactional inbox placement is the only priority and budget isn't. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Postmark](/blog/reloop-vs-postmark).

## 4. Amazon SES: cheapest raw sending pipe

[Amazon SES](https://aws.amazon.com/ses/) is a sending pipe at $0.10 per 1,000 emails. Resend itself sends through SES.

**Pricing:** $200 in AWS Free Tier credits for the first 6 months, then $0.10 per 1,000 plus $0.12 per GB of outbound data. About $10 for 100k.

**Strengths:** the lowest per-email price, IAM, SNS and Lambda integration.

**Weaknesses:** you build the product around it: sandbox exit, bounce and complaint handling through SNS, suppression, templates, logs and inbound.

**Best for:** AWS-native teams with engineering time to build what Resend gave them. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Amazon SES](/blog/reloop-vs-aws-ses).

## 5. SendGrid: best for enterprise procurement

[SendGrid](https://sendgrid.com), part of Twilio, is the incumbent for large organizations.

**Pricing:** a 60-day trial at 100 a day. Essentials is $19.95 for 50k and $34.95 for 100k; Pro, with dedicated IPs, subusers and SSO, starts at $89.95.

**Strengths:** enterprise compliance, subuser management, full Handlebars logic in templates.

**Weaknesses:** no lasting free tier, marketing on separate plans, inbound is a parse webhook.

**Best for:** companies already inside Twilio that need procurement checkboxes. Full comparison: [Reloop vs SendGrid](/blog/reloop-vs-sendgrid).

## 6. Mailgun: best for routing and validation

[Mailgun](https://www.mailgun.com) is one of the original developer email APIs, with inbound routes, webhooks and email validation.

**Pricing:** a trial at 100 a day. Basic is $15 for 10,000; Foundation is $35 for 50,000, with overage from $1.30 per 1,000.

**Strengths:** regex inbound routes, detailed logs, validation built in.

**Weaknesses:** no real free tier, validation is an add-on, marketing sits in a separate product.

**Best for:** teams that need inbound routing rules and validation from one vendor. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Mailgun](/blog/reloop-vs-mailgun).

---

## 7. MailerSend: best no-code builder

[MailerSend](https://www.mailersend.com) is a developer API with the most complete no-code side here.

**Pricing:** free for 500 emails a month. Hobby is $7 for 5,000; Starter is $35 for 50k and $68 for 100k.

**Strengths:** drag-and-drop builder, SMS, email verification and split testing.

**Weaknesses:** small free tier, inbound is forwarded rather than stored, and each forward counts toward your quota.

**Best for:** teams where non-developers edit emails. Full comparison: [Reloop vs MailerSend](/blog/reloop-vs-mailersend).

## 8. Cloudflare Email Service: best for apps on Workers

[Cloudflare Email Service](https://developers.cloudflare.com/email-service/) adds sending to Cloudflare's Email Routing. From a Worker it's one binding and one `send()` call.

**Pricing:** sending to any recipient needs Workers Paid ($5 a month minimum), which includes 3,000 emails, then $0.35 per 1,000. About $21 at 50k and $39 at 100k.

**Strengths:** cheap at high volume, one `send()` call from a Worker, and unlimited inbound through Email Routing.

**Weaknesses:** sending is in public beta, your domain must use Cloudflare DNS, and it's transactional only with no templates, contacts or campaigns.

**Best for:** apps that live entirely on Workers and send only transactional mail. Full comparison: [Reloop vs Cloudflare Email Service](/blog/reloop-vs-cloudflare-email).

## How to choose

- **"We like Resend's API but the bill is growing."** Reloop. Same shape, $10 at 50k and $20 at 100k.
- **"We need open source."** Reloop (Apache 2.0, its own MTA) or useSend (AGPL, on SES).
- **"We need agents or humans to read and reply to inbound mail."** Reloop, the only option here with stored mailboxes, MCP and IMAP.
- **"Deliverability for receipts is everything."** Postmark.
- **"We're on AWS and have engineers to spare."** Amazon SES.
- **"Procurement needs a big vendor."** SendGrid.
- **"Our app runs on Cloudflare Workers."** Cloudflare Email Service.

## Switching from Resend

Most of the work is DNS, not code.

1. **Add your domain** in the new provider and publish its DKIM record. Merge its SPF include into your existing SPF record; never publish a second one.
2. **Swap the client.** For Reloop, replace `resend.emails.send` with `reloop.mail.send` and rename `replyTo` to `reply_to` and `scheduledAt` to `scheduled_at`.
3. **Move templates.** React Email components keep working. Stored templates in Reloop use triple braces: `{{{first_name}}}`.
4. **Import suppressions** so you don't email addresses that bounced or unsubscribed.
5. **Point webhooks** at a new handler. Reloop's events (`email.delivered`, `email.bounced` and so on) are signed with HMAC-SHA256 in the `Reloop-Signature` header.
6. **Ramp traffic** from 10% to 100% over one to two weeks, then remove Resend's DNS records.
