## Churn is usually predictable — if you're watching

Most churn doesn't happen suddenly. It follows a pattern: decreased logins → fewer API calls → silence → cancellation. Email sequences, triggered by these behavioral signals, can interrupt that pattern before the user is mentally gone.

## The three churn windows

### 1. Day 1–14: Activation churn
Users who never activate are the most likely to churn. Identify your activation event (first email sent, first domain verified, first API call) and build a sequence around it.

- **Day 2**: "One thing to try today" — single CTA to the activation step
- **Day 5**: "Here's how [similar company] got started" — social proof
- **Day 10**: "Need help?" — personal outreach from a founder

### 2. Day 30–90: Engagement churn
Users who activated but stopped engaging. These are often users who got distracted or didn't see enough value.

- Trigger: No login in 14 days
- Email: Surface a specific piece of value they haven't discovered yet
- Follow-up: Offer a 1:1 call or product walkthrough

### 3. Day 90+: Win-back
Users who've fully disengaged. Low-probability but still worth a single, honest outreach.

- Don't oversell. Acknowledge the silence.
- Offer something concrete: a feature they requested, a pricing adjustment, a migration path.
- One email. If no response, let them go.

## What to measure

Track churn rate segmented by whether a user received the at-risk sequence vs. didn't. This lets you A/B test the sequences themselves over time.
