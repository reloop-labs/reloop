---
title: Suppression
description: Why contacts are automatically suppressed after hard bounces and spam complaints, and what to do about it.
---

**Suppression** stops Reloop from sending email to a contact whose address is permanently unreachable or who reported your message as spam. Suppression protects your domain reputation and keeps your contact list accurate.

When a contact is suppressed, their profile shows a **suppression reason** and **suppressed at** timestamp. Suppressed contacts are skipped on future sends until the suppression is resolved.

## How suppression appears in the dashboard

On the **Contacts** list, suppressed contacts are identifiable by their status and suppression metadata.

On the **contact detail page**:

1. Open **Contacts** and click the contact row.
2. Check the profile header and status area for suppression indicators.
3. Review **Email activity** on the same page — a bounce or complaint event is usually the trigger.

If a contact is suppressed, see [why it happened](#suppression-reasons) below before attempting to send again.

## Suppression reasons

| Reason | API value | What happened | What Reloop does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hard bounce | `hard_bounce` | The recipient's mail server permanently rejected the message (invalid mailbox, domain does not exist, policy block). | Contact is suppressed automatically. Future sends to this address are blocked. |
| Spam complaint | `spam_complaint` | The recipient clicked "Report spam" or equivalent at their email provider. | Contact is suppressed automatically. Future sends to this address are blocked. |

<Callout type="warning" title="Do not email suppressed contacts">
  Sending to hard-bounced or complaining addresses hurts deliverability for your entire domain. Treat suppression as permanent unless you have a documented reason to clear it (e.g. the contact confirmed a typo fix on a new address).
</Callout>

## Hard bounce — why it happens

A **hard bounce** means the receiving server will not accept mail to that address again. Common causes:

- **Invalid address** — typo in the email (`user@gmial.com`), deleted mailbox, or former employee.
- **Unknown user** — the local part or domain does not exist.
- **Policy rejection** — the provider blocks mail to that account permanently.

### What you see in email activity

On the contact detail page, **Email activity** shows the message with a **failed** or **bounced** status. The timeline includes delivery events leading up to the hard bounce.

### What you should do

1. **Verify the address** — if it was a typo, create or update the contact with the correct email (as a new contact if the old address is invalid).
2. **Do not retry the bad address** — repeated sends to hard bounces damage sender reputation.
3. **Clean your list at import** — validate emails before adding large batches. See the [Migration guide](/docs/learn/contacts/migration-guide) for API-based imports.

## Spam complaint — why it happens

A **spam complaint** means the recipient explicitly marked your message as spam. Common causes:

- **Unexpected mail** — they do not remember signing up.
- **Too frequent sends** — marketing volume exceeded expectations.
- **Irrelevant content** — message did not match what they opted into.
- **Missing or unclear unsubscribe** — they used "Report spam" instead of your preference link.

### What you see in email activity

The activity timeline shows the message and a **spam**-related status or event when the complaint was recorded.

### What you should do

1. **Never send marketing to complainers again** — suppression is automatic and should stay in place.
2. **Review consent and frequency** — audit [channels](/docs/learn/contacts/channels) and [subscriptions](/docs/learn/contacts/subscriptions) for the cohort that complained.
3. **Use the preference center** — give contacts channel-level opt-out before they complain. See [Subscriptions](/docs/learn/contacts/subscriptions).

## Suppression vs. other contact states

| State | Set by | Sends allowed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `subscribed` | You or the contact | Yes (respecting channel opt-outs) |
| `unsubscribed` | Contact or you (global opt-out) | No (for mail that honors unsubscribe) |
| `blocked` | You (manual) | No |
| Suppressed (`hard_bounce` / `spam_complaint`) | Reloop (automatic from delivery events) | No |

A contact can be `subscribed` in status but still **suppressed** if a bounce or complaint was recorded — suppression takes precedence for delivery.

## API fields

When you retrieve a contact, suppression appears on the response:

```json
{
  "id": "cont_123456789",
  "email": "user@example.com",
  "status": "subscribed",
  "suppressionReason": "hard_bounce",
  "suppressedAt": "2026-03-15T10:00:00Z"
}
```

| Field | Type | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `suppressionReason` | `"hard_bounce"` \| `"spam_complaint"` \| `null` | Why the contact was suppressed, or `null` if not suppressed |
| `suppressedAt` | ISO 8601 string \| `null` | When suppression was applied |

```typescript
const { response, error } = await reloop.contacts.get("cont_123456789");
if (error) throw error;

if (response.suppressionReason) {
  console.log("Suppressed:", response.suppressionReason, response.suppressedAt);
}
```
